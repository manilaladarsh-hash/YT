## 3. Build a balanced list, and a budget

**Advice.** Aim for 8 to 15 applications. Choose by research fit first and prestige second. A program where two or more faculty work on what you want is a stronger application than a famous name where nobody does.

- **Fit test:** for each program, name two faculty members and one recent paper from each that you could discuss. If you can't, it isn't a fit yet.
- **Spread:** mix programs where your profile is typical of admits with a few reaches. Use each department's own descriptions (several state typical GPAs or class sizes on their pages) rather than forum rumours.
- **Cost reality:** the median international application fee in this Atlas is **{{MEDIAN_USD}} USD (about {{MEDIAN_USD_INR}})** for US programs and **{{MEDIAN_CAD}} CAD (about {{MEDIAN_CAD_INR}})** for Canadian programs, where stated. Ten US applications therefore cost roughly **{{TEN_US_INR}}** before test-score fees. Many UK, European and Australian programs charge no application fee (Sections 6–8). Rupee figures use the Bank of Canada rate of {{FX_DATE}} (1 USD ≈ ₹{{USD_INR}}), and your bank adds a margin.
- **Budget it** in the **Budget** tab, which converts every currency to rupees with an editable rate. Add score-sending and transcript costs from the official sites; we don't estimate those for you.

## 4. English tests in 2026: what changed and who waives

**Verified change:** TOEFL iBT moved to a new **1-6 score scale on January 21, 2026**. Programs now list two minimums, one per scale (e.g., UBC: 90 on the old scale or 4.5 on the new one). Check which scale your score is on.

There are four kinds of waiver rule. Which kind a program uses decides whether an English-medium Indian degree is enough:

1. **Friendly to an English-medium Indian degree ("Yes" in the Atlas):** {{ENGLISH_YES_LIST}}. Most of these look at your university's language of instruction. Some ask for an official letter or transcript notation (e.g., Toronto), and some for documents sent to the department (McGill). UT Austin is different: its exempt-country list includes India itself.
2. **Conditional / list-based.** Exemption only if your institution meets a specific test. Alberta publishes a list that includes the IITs (all campuses), IISc and the University of Hyderabad but **not** the IISERs. Waterloo accepts only institutions listed on WHED with English as the *only* language of instruction, and explicitly rejects medium-of-instruction letters. Queen's lets you *request* an exemption after a recent year of English-medium study. In this Atlas: {{ENGLISH_CONDITIONAL_LIST}}.
3. **No waiver for an English-medium Indian degree ("No"):** {{ENGLISH_NO_LIST}}. These either use an "English-primary country" list that India is not on (e.g., UIUC, Berkeley, SFU, Calgary, and Cambridge via the UK government's list), or require a test from everyone (Georgia Tech).
4. **Not stated** on the pages checked: ask the program before you assume.

**Advice:** if even one program on your list needs a score, book the test now. Score delivery takes time; Waterloo, for example, says it takes 4-6 weeks for official scores to reach it. Also check speaking minimums. Several programs tie teaching-assistant eligibility (and so part of your funding) to a separate, higher speaking score.

## 6. The UK: funding competitions, and English rules for Indian degrees

The {{N_UK}} UK programs in this Atlas mostly work like this:
- you apply to a department, usually naming a project or a supervisor;
- funding is decided in separate studentship and scholarship competitions, which close well before the final admission deadline.

{{UK_TABLE}}

**Apply by the funding deadline, not the final one.** Examples:
- Imperial's President's PhD Scholarship rounds: Nov 2, Jan 11 and Mar 1.
- Warwick's Chancellor's Scholarship: Dec 7.
- Cambridge: Dec 8.
- Durham: Dec 15.
- Bristol: before Jan 18.
- Edinburgh: Jan 22.
- QMUL studentships: Jan 28.
- Glasgow studentships: Jan 31.

**Funding for international students is limited.**
- St Andrews says research councils permit only 30% of their funded places to go to international applicants.
- Warwick says its funded projects are in most cases limited to UK nationals.
- Imperial says studentships for overseas applicants are very restricted.

Without funding, international tuition is high: Glasgow lists £33,708 a year for 2027/28. Apply for every scholarship you're eligible for, and check each university's India page for country-specific schemes (advice).

**No application fee at many UK universities:** {{UK_NO_FEE_LIST}}. Cambridge charges £20 per applicant per year for up to 7 doctoral applications.

**English is the biggest UK trap for Indian applicants.**
- **No route without a test.** Most UK universities exempt only degrees from UKVI "majority English-speaking" countries, and India isn't one of them. In this Atlas, no exemption route for an Indian degree: {{UK_ENGLISH_NO_LIST}}.
- **India-specific evidence accepted elsewhere:**
  - QMUL accepts at least 70% in the English component of CBSE or ISC Class XII.
  - Royal Holloway accepts Class 12 English of at least 80% for research degrees, or a degree from a listed Indian university.
  - Bristol and St Andrews accept an official medium-of-instruction letter.
  - Edinburgh accepts English-taught degrees only from institutions on its approved list.

  In this Atlas, some India route exists: {{UK_ENGLISH_ROUTE_LIST}}.
- **Advice:** if your list mixes both kinds, book IELTS or TOEFL anyway. It's the only evidence every UK university accepts.

**A strong bachelor's is often enough.** Most UK programs ask for a first or 2:1 honours degree or the international equivalent: {{UK_BACHELORS_LIST}}. Check the university's India page, though: Royal Holloway's says Indian applicants are considered for a PhD after a master's.

**Name a supervisor, and plan for ATAS.**
- Glasgow, King's College London and QMUL want you to contact or name a supervisor before applying; others recommend it.
- King's College London notes that successful applicants whose nationality isn't exempt need an **ATAS** security clearance certificate, which takes time. Check the UK government's ATAS page as soon as you get an offer.

## 7. The rest of Europe: positions, calls and master's-first rules

The {{N_EU}} programs in continental Europe and Ireland ({{EU_COUNTRIES}}) use two very different ways in.

1. **Calls with a deadline.** You apply to a graduate school or program by a published date, often followed by interviews. Examples:
   - Nov 1: IMPRS Quantum Dynamics, and Heidelberg's HGSFP portal.
   - Nov 3: IMPRS Astronomy (Heidelberg).
   - Nov 30: IMPRS Quantum Materials (Dresden).
   - Dec 1: the Max Planck Schools of Photonics and Matter to Life, which both take bachelor's graduates (see below).
   - Spring: SISSA's exam-based call.
   - Oct 31 and Apr 30: EPFL's two rounds.
2. **Apply for a paid position, or agree a supervisor first.** There is no central committee: PhD places are advertised as jobs, or a professor must accept you before you apply. Sweden (Stockholm, KTH, Chalmers, Uppsala), Denmark (Copenhagen), the Netherlands (Leiden, Amsterdam, Delft), Switzerland (ETH Zurich, Zurich), TU Munich, ICFO and Trinity College Dublin work this way. In this Atlas, position- or supervisor-based: {{EU_POSITION_LIST}}.

{{EU_TABLE}}

**Fees are often low or zero.**
- **No application fee:** {{EU_NO_FEE_LIST}}.
- **Small fees:** Zurich (UZH) charges CHF 100.
- **Watch for tuition instead.** From January 2027, Copenhagen charges PhD tuition of DKK 85,000 a year, which your funding must cover.
- **German fees are appearing.** The Max Planck School of Photonics notes that FAU Erlangen, one of its teaching universities, charges students from non-EU countries €100 per application.

**A master's is usually expected first.** In this Atlas: {{EU_MASTERS_LIST}}. Bachelor's holders can enter at: {{EU_BACHELORS_LIST}}. Other routes:
- Heidelberg (HGSFP and IMPRS Astronomy) has 4+4 tracks for excellent candidates with a 4-year bachelor's.
- EPFL accepts 4- or 5-year bachelor's holders who explain their education in the Statement of Objectives.
- Swedish rules (KTH, Stockholm) accept 240 credits including at least 60 at advanced level, so a 4-year degree with enough advanced courses can qualify.

If you hold a 5-year BS-MS, ask each program how it assesses your degree, and keep the reply.

**Funding usually means a job contract.**
- **Sweden:** funding for the whole doctorate is a condition of admission, and doctoral students are usually salaried. KTH quotes SEK 33,000–37,100 a month.
- **Funded-only admission elsewhere:** Copenhagen, ICFO and Paris-Saclay admit only students who are funded.
- **Positions:** Leiden's PhD projects are 4-year paid positions, and ETH doctorates are, with few exceptions, linked to a position.

In this Atlas, paid or typically funded: {{EU_FUNDED_LIST}}; competitive: {{EU_COMPETITIVE_LIST}}.

**English rules for Indian degrees are often friendlier than in the UK.**
- **Friendly:**
  - Aalto lists India for English-medium degrees.
  - Stockholm and IMPRS Solar System count an English-taught degree.
  - TU Delft exempts English-taught master's degrees in principle.
  - IMPRS Astronomy (Heidelberg) and Copenhagen assess English at interview instead of by test.
- **School-level English counts in Sweden:** at KTH and Chalmers, your CBSE or CISCE Class XII English grades can meet the requirement.

### Two routes beyond university departments

**Max Planck Schools (Germany).**
- You apply from September 1 to December 1, 2026 for a fall 2027 start, through one central portal. The Atlas covers Photonics and Matter to Life.
- Bachelor's graduates are welcome as well as master's graduates. Bachelor's holders start with an integrated master's phase on a stipend (about €1,565 a month), then move to a salaried PhD contract (TVöD/TV-L E13).
- Admission includes full funding for up to five years, and there are no tuition fees.
- Matter to Life charges no application fee and accepts an English-taught previous degree as proof of English. The Photonics FAQ says the GRE is not required and won't be evaluated.

**Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Networks (MSCA-DN, EU-funded).**
- These are individual paid PhD positions across Europe, open to **any nationality**. You must **not already have a doctorate** when you're recruited.
- **Mobility rule:** you must not have lived or had your main activity (work, studies) in the recruiting organisation's country for more than 12 months in the 36 months before your recruitment date. Applying from India, you'll usually meet it. Check it if you've recently studied or worked in that country.
- The EU pays a living allowance and a mobility allowance. Each position has its own deadline and its own application.
- **How to find them:** search EURAXESS (euraxess.ec.europa.eu → Jobs) for "MSCA Doctoral Network" plus your field, and check weekly; new calls appear all year.

**APS certificate (Germany).** Many German programs and the German student visa route ask Indian applicants for an APS certificate. In this Atlas, Matter to Life explicitly asks for APS verification for Indian certificates. Check each program's page and aps-india.de, and if you need it, apply early.

**Advice.**
- In position-based systems, your supervisor email (Section 10) *is* the application. Check the vacancy pages weekly; KTH, for example, announces positions nine times a year.
- Allow time after an offer for contracts and permits. EPFL says about 3 months is usually needed for visas and work permits.

### Master's-first rules

Many Canadian PhD programs expect a master's degree. A BSc holder usually starts in the MSc and may transfer to the PhD later. In this Atlas, "Master's usually required": {{MASTERS_FIRST_LIST}}. Direct or either route: {{EITHER_LIST}}.

**Important for 5-year integrated BS-MS students (IISER, IISc, IIT and others):** whether your degree counts as a master's equivalent is decided by each university's credential assessment. **Ask each program in writing.** Don't assume either way. Put the question in your first email to the graduate office and keep the reply.

### Study permits in 2026: two changes worth knowing (facts, not advice)

- **No provincial letter for graduate students.** Starting January 1, 2026, if you'll attend a master's or doctoral degree program at a public institution, you don't need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL or TAL) for your study permit. Quebec still has its own requirements. [canada.ca: graduate students](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/graduate-student/how-to-apply.html)
- **About 2-week processing for PhD students.** IRCC says it is processing doctoral students' applications (and their family members') in 2 weeks. To qualify, you must:
  - apply from outside Canada, online, with a complete application;
  - give biometrics within 2 weeks of your instruction letter.

  The clock starts once your university verifies your letter of acceptance. [canada.ca: faster processing](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/graduate-student/faster-processing.html)

These rules change. Check canada.ca on the day you apply. This kit is not immigration advice.

### Funding is stated more often

Canadian programs often publish guaranteed minimums (e.g., UBC at least CAD 40,000 a year for years 1-4; Alberta CAD 100,000 over four years; McMaster at least CAD 21,000 after tuition and fees). Note whether a figure is before or after tuition.

## 10. Supervisor emails that get answered (advice)

**Find people, not just programs.** Use faculty pages, the group's own website (look for "accepting students"), and papers from the last two years. Shortlist 2-4 people per university.

**The 6-sentence email:**

> **Subject:** Prospective PhD student (Fall 2027): [specific topic] in your group
>
> 1. Who you are: degree, institution, expected graduation.
> 2. The specific work of theirs you read, and one genuine connection to your own work. Don't add a quiz-style question they'd have to research to answer.
> 3. Your most relevant research experience and what *you* did in it.
> 4. One skill or result that would help their group (a technique, code, measurement).
> 5. The ask: "Are you planning to take new PhD students for Fall 2027, and would you be open to my application?"
> 6. Thanks, with CV and transcript attached as PDFs named Lastname_CV.pdf.

**Cadence:** one polite follow-up after about 10 days if you get no reply, then move on. Log every email in the **Supervisor outreach** tab; it calculates the follow-up date for you.

**Don'ts:**
- no mass emails with swapped names;
- no long life stories;
- no attachments over a few MB;
- no generic praise ("your esteemed research");
- no request for a video call in the first email. If they're interested, they'll suggest one.

## 11. Statement of purpose: a physics framework (advice)

Programs set their own length rules. Alberta, for example, wants **500 words or less on a single page**. Read each program's prompt and obey it exactly.

**A structure that works for research PhDs:**

1. **Opening (2-3 sentences):** the physics question that drives you, stated concretely ("how disorder changes spin dynamics in…"), not "I have loved physics since childhood".
2. **Research experience (40-50% of the text):** for each project, give the question, *your* contribution, the method, the result, and what you learned, including what went wrong and how you fixed it.
3. **Skills and preparation:** techniques, instruments, code, and advanced courses that map onto their work.
4. **Why this program:** two named faculty and why, plus facilities or centres that matter for your plans.
5. **Goals:** where the PhD leads and why this training fits.

**Check before you submit:** every sentence should be true and specific, and you should be able to defend it in an interview. Write it yourself. Never use purchased or ghost-written statements.

## 12. Recommendation letters: the request kit (advice)

- **Who:** research supervisors first, then instructors of advanced courses who know your work. Programs differ on number: Toronto wants **exactly two** (no more), while UIUC, Columbia and Maryland list three.
- **When:** ask at least 4-6 weeks before your earliest deadline, and send portal invitations early.
- **What to send (one email, one PDF):** your shortlist with deadlines and portal links, CV, draft SOP, a "brag sheet" (projects, dates, what you did, skills shown), and a reminder of specific moments they saw your work.
- **Reminders:** a friendly note 10 days and again 3 days before each deadline. The **Recommenders** tab tracks status per program.

## 15. Money, safety and what this kit does not cover

- **Scams:** be wary of anyone who guarantees admission, sells "special" fee-waiver codes, or asks for your portal passwords. Real waiver codes come from the program itself; Penn State, for example, publishes its own code on its admissions page.
- **Paying fees:** many portals take Visa or Mastercard credit cards. Some accept debit cards selected as "credit" (UBC says this). Enable international online transactions with your bank before the deadline week.
- **Visas and study permits:** out of scope. Use only official sources: travel.state.gov for US student visas, canada.ca (IRCC) for Canadian study permits, and for Europe the host university's international office and the national immigration authority it points you to. Paid immigration advice in Canada is a regulated activity, and this kit is not that.

## 16. How this data was built (and how to report an error)

- Each of the {{N}} programs was checked against its own department and graduate-school pages on {{CHECKED}}. Every value stores the exact sentence it came from, the URL and the date. A script confirms that each quote appears verbatim on the fetched page.
- Research and drafting were AI-assisted. Every published value is tied to an official quote, and uncertain items are labelled *Not stated*, *Conditional*, *year inferred* or *sources conflict* instead of guessed.
- A weekly automated check re-reads the cited pages and flags quotes that disappear. Updates are sent to buyers as new versions at no charge.
- Some well-known programs couldn't be verified on the check date because their sites blocked automated checks or were down for maintenance. They are not in this edition: Oxford, UCL, Manchester, Michigan, ISTA Austria, Vienna, Göttingen's central pages, Geneva, KU Leuven, ENS Paris (ICFP) and the University of Barcelona. They'll be added once their pages can be verified.
- **Found something wrong or changed?** Reply to your purchase email with the program and the page link. Confirmed corrections ship in the next update.
