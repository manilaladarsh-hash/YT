## 3. Build a balanced list, and a budget

**Advice.** Aim for 8 to 15 applications. Choose by research fit first and prestige second. A program where two or more faculty work on what you want is a stronger application than a famous name where nobody does.

- **Fit test:** for each program, name two faculty members and one recent paper from each that you could discuss. If you can't, it isn't a fit yet.
- **Spread:** mix programs where your profile is typical of admits with a few reaches. Use each department's own descriptions (several state typical GPAs or class sizes on their pages) rather than forum rumours.
- **Cost reality:** where a fee is stated, the median application fee in this Atlas is **{{MEDIAN_USD}} USD (about {{MEDIAN_USD_INR}})** in the US, **{{MEDIAN_GBP}} GBP (about {{MEDIAN_GBP_INR}})** in the UK and **{{MEDIAN_CAD}} CAD (about {{MEDIAN_CAD_INR}})** in Canada. Ten US applications therefore cost roughly **{{TEN_US_INR}}** before test-score fees. **{{NO_FEE_COUNT}} programs charge no fee at all**, and {{WAIVER_ROUTE_COUNT}} more offer a waiver route to international applicants (Section 14). Rupee figures use {{FX_SOURCE}} ({{FX_DATE}}; 1 USD ≈ ₹{{USD_INR}}), and your bank adds a margin.
- **Budget it** in My plan, or in the workbook's **Budget** tab, which converts every currency to rupees with an editable rate. Add score-sending and transcript costs from the official sites; we don't estimate those.

## 4. English tests: who waives them for an English-medium Indian degree

**Verified change:** TOEFL iBT moved to a new **1–6 score scale on January 21, 2026**. Programs now often list two minimums, one per scale (e.g., 90 on the old scale or 4.5 on the new one). Check which scale your score is on. Many universities also list an IELTS Academic minimum, and the Atlas shows both.

The English rule is usually set **once for the whole university**, so it is the same for physics, CS or biology there, unless a department adds its own higher bar. Across the {{N_UNIS}} universities in this Atlas:

1. **An English-medium Indian degree is accepted ("Yes"), at {{ENGLISH_YES_COUNT}} universities**, including {{ENGLISH_YES_LIST}}. Most look at your university's language of instruction; many want an official medium-of-instruction (MOI) letter or a transcript note. A few list India itself as exempt.
2. **Conditional or list-based, at {{ENGLISH_CONDITIONAL_COUNT}} universities**, including {{ENGLISH_CONDITIONAL_LIST}}. The exemption depends on a specific test: your institution must be on a named list, all instruction must have been in English, the degree must be recent, or you need a minimum Class XII English mark. (For example, Alberta's list includes the IITs and IISc but not the IISERs; QMUL accepts 70% in CBSE or ISC Class XII English.) Read the quote.
3. **No waiver for an Indian degree ("No"), at {{ENGLISH_NO_COUNT}} universities**, including {{ENGLISH_NO_LIST}}. These use an "English-speaking country" list that India is not on (common in the US and the UK), or they require a test from everyone.
4. **Not stated** on the pages checked: ask the graduate office before you assume.

**Advice:** if even one program on your list needs a score, book the test now. Score delivery takes weeks. Also check speaking minimums: many US programs tie teaching-assistant eligibility, and so part of your funding, to a separate speaking score.

## 6. How your field admits PhD students

Rules differ more **between fields** than most applicants expect. Each line below comes from the programs in this Atlas; the notes under each group are advice.

{{FIELD_TABLE}}

*Bachelor's OK = direct entry from a bachelor's, or either route. Supervisor first = a supervisor's agreement is required before or during admission. Percentages count the programs where the page states the rule.*

### Physical sciences: physics, astronomy, chemistry, materials

- **US programs admit a cohort** through a committee. You usually choose an adviser in year 1 (chemistry and materials departments often run formal rotations or lab visits). The Physics and Chemistry GRE Subject tests still matter at some programs (Section 5).
- **Europe and Australia** usually admit you to a group or an advertised position, often after a master's. Max Planck graduate schools (IMPRS) and the Max Planck Schools run central calls with fixed deadlines.
- **Advice:** in your statement, name instruments, techniques and code you have actually used (e.g., XRD, DFT, cleanroom, Python analysis pipelines). Lab skills transfer.

### Mathematical sciences: mathematics, statistics and biostatistics

- **US math PhDs are mostly funded through teaching**, and admit straight from a bachelor's. Expect qualifying exams in years 1 to 2. The **GRE Mathematics Subject test** is required or recommended at some programs; check the table in Section 5.
- **Statistics and biostatistics** often ask for linear algebra, real analysis and programming (R or Python) as prerequisites. Biostatistics is frequently in a school of public health with its own fee and deadline.
- **Advice:** pure-math applicants should show proof-based courses and any reading or research projects. Statistics applicants should show both theory and real data work.

### Computing: computer science, data science, ML and AI

- **CS is the most competitive field in this Atlas for international applicants.** There is no GRE Subject test; most US CS departments have made the General GRE optional or dropped it.
- **Funding is often tied to a research adviser** (RA positions), so naming faculty whose work matches yours matters even in committee-admitted US programs.
- **"Data science" PhDs** are newer. Many universities run them inside CS, statistics or a dedicated institute; the Atlas lists them under Data Science, ML & AI only where a separate program or official track exists.
- **Europe:** most CS PhDs are paid positions with a supervisor. AI-specific doctoral networks (for example the ELLIS PhD program, and national AI graduate schools) run their own calls. Check their official pages for this cycle's dates.
- **Advice:** a public GitHub or project page, and a paper or preprint if you have one, carry more weight than coursework lists.

### Life sciences: biology, biomedical sciences, neuroscience

- **US biology is usually an "umbrella" admission.** You apply once to a large program, are interviewed (often January–February, frequently with travel or virtual visits), do 2 to 3 lab rotations, then choose a lab. Funding is usually guaranteed. The Atlas shows the umbrella under its main field, with the other fields it covers in brackets.
- **Biomedical sciences** programs are often in a medical school, with their own portal and fee.
- **Neuroscience** programs may sit in biology, psychology or medicine; check which.
- **UK, Europe and Australia** usually admit you to one project or supervisor (UK four-year doctoral training programmes, called DTPs, recruit cohorts with set deadlines).
- **Advice:** research experience beats grades here. Describe your own role in each project, the techniques, and what you would do next.

### Earth and environment: geoscience, atmospheric, ocean and environmental science

- **Even in the US, admission is often adviser-driven**: many departments expect you to contact potential advisers first, and funding comes from their grants.
- Fieldwork and quantitative skills (GIS, remote sensing, modelling, coding) are strong signals.
- **Advice:** email 2 to 3 faculty early (Section 11). Ask whether they have funded positions for Fall 2027.

### Engineering: electrical/computer, mechanical/aerospace, chemical, civil, biomedical

- **US engineering PhDs are often RA-funded by an adviser.** Some departments guarantee funding; many fund only students an adviser takes on. The Atlas funding value says which.
- **Many US engineering departments admit bachelor's holders directly to the PhD**, but some route you through an MS first.
- **Biomedical engineering** often runs rotations like biology; **ECE** is large and competitive, much like CS.
- **Advice:** contact faculty whose recent papers match your project experience, and say clearly which methods you can contribute.

## 7. The UK: funding competitions, and English rules for Indian degrees

The {{N_UK}} UK programs in this Atlas mostly work like this:
- you apply to a department, usually naming a project or a supervisor;
- funding is decided in separate studentship and scholarship competitions, which close well before the final admission deadline.

**Apply by the funding deadline, not the final one.** Examples from this Atlas:
- Imperial's President's PhD Scholarship rounds: Nov 2, Jan 11 and Mar 1.
- Warwick's Chancellor's Scholarship: Dec 7.
- Cambridge: Dec 8 for most funding competitions.
- UK doctoral training programmes (DTPs and CDTs) in biology, environment and engineering often recruit between December and February.

**Funding for international students is limited.** St Andrews says research councils permit only 30% of their funded places to go to international applicants, and Imperial says studentships for overseas applicants are very restricted. Without funding, international tuition is high: Glasgow lists £33,708 a year for 2027/28. Apply for every scholarship you're eligible for, and check each university's India page for country-specific schemes (advice).

**Application fees:** {{UK_NO_FEE_COUNT}} of the {{N_UK}} UK programs here charge no application fee. Cambridge charges a small fee per applicant per year.

**English is the biggest UK trap for Indian applicants.** Most UK universities exempt only degrees from UKVI "majority English-speaking" countries, and India isn't one of them. UK universities in this Atlas with **no** exemption route for an Indian degree: {{UK_ENGLISH_NO_LIST}}. Some India-specific route exists at: {{UK_ENGLISH_ROUTE_LIST}}. **Advice:** if your list mixes both kinds, book IELTS or TOEFL anyway. It's the only evidence every UK university accepts.

**Plan for ATAS.** Many UK physical-science and engineering PhDs need an **ATAS** security clearance certificate for applicants whose nationality isn't exempt (Indian nationals need one). It takes weeks. Check the UK government's ATAS page as soon as you get an offer.

## 8. The rest of Europe: positions, calls and master's-first rules

The {{N_EU}} programs in continental Europe and Ireland ({{EU_COUNTRIES}}) use two very different ways in:

1. **Calls with a deadline.** You apply to a graduate school by a published date, often followed by interviews. Examples: Max Planck graduate schools (IMPRS) and the Max Planck Schools (apply Sep 1 to Dec 1, 2026, open to bachelor's graduates), EPFL's doctoral rounds, SISSA's calls, and German cluster graduate schools.
2. **Apply for a paid position, or agree a supervisor first.** There is no central committee: PhD places are advertised as jobs, or a professor must accept you before you apply. This is the norm in Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland, the Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland (ETH, UZH) and much of Germany and Austria. {{EU_POSITION_COUNT}} of the {{N_EU}} programs here are per-position or supervisor-first.

**Fees are often low or zero.** {{EU_NO_FEE_COUNT}} of these programs charge no application fee. Watch for tuition instead: from January 2027, Copenhagen charges PhD tuition of DKK 85,000 a year, which your funding must cover.

**A master's is usually expected first.** {{EU_MASTERS_PCT}} of European programs that state an entry route expect a master's. Bachelor's routes exist (Max Planck Schools, some 4+4 tracks in Germany, EPFL for 4-year degrees, Swedish rules that accept 240 credits with 60 at advanced level). **Advice:** if you hold a 5-year BS-MS, ask each program how it assesses your degree, and keep the reply.

**Funding usually means a job contract.** In Sweden, funding for the whole doctorate is a condition of admission and doctoral students are usually salaried. Dutch, Danish, Swiss and Nordic positions are typically paid employment.

**Two routes beyond departments:**
- **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Networks (MSCA-DN, EU-funded):** individual paid PhD positions across Europe in every STEM field, open to **any nationality** if you don't already have a doctorate. **Mobility rule:** you must not have lived or had your main activity in the recruiting country for more than 12 months in the 36 months before recruitment. Search EURAXESS (euraxess.ec.europa.eu → Jobs) for "MSCA Doctoral Network" plus your field, weekly.
- **APS certificate (Germany):** many German programs and the German student visa route ask Indian applicants for an APS certificate. Check each program's page and aps-india.de, and apply early if you need it.

**Advice.** In position-based systems your supervisor email (Section 11) *is* the application. Check vacancy pages weekly, and allow about 3 months after an offer for contracts and permits.

## 9. Australia and New Zealand: supervisor first, scholarship rounds

The {{N_AU}} programs in this Atlas ({{AU_COUNTRIES}}) share a pattern:

- **Find a supervisor before you apply.** Most Australian and New Zealand universities require a supervisor's support before or with your application. {{AU_SUP_PCT}} of the programs here that state the rule require it.
- **No application fee for research degrees** at {{AU_NO_FEE_COUNT}} of the {{N_AU}} programs here.
- **Scholarships run in rounds and are competitive.** An offer of admission doesn't guarantee a scholarship. The Australian academic year starts around February–March, so the main international rounds for 2027 starts may already be closed; mid-year and later rounds exist (the Atlas shows each university's next date).
- **Entry needs research experience:** usually an honours or 4-year bachelor's with a research component, or a master's. Advice: a 4-year BS or a BS-MS with a thesis fits these rules better than a 3-year BSc.

## 10. Canada: supervisors and master's-first rules

- **Supervisor first.** Admission often depends on a professor agreeing to take you. {{CA_SUP_PCT}} of the Canadian programs here that state the rule require or recommend contacting a supervisor first.
- **Master's first.** Many Canadian PhD programs expect a master's; a bachelor's holder usually starts in the MSc and may transfer later. {{CA_MASTERS_PCT}} of the Canadian programs that state an entry route expect a master's.
- **5-year integrated BS-MS holders (IISER, IISc, IIT and others):** whether your degree counts as a master's equivalent is decided by each university's credential assessment. **Ask in writing** and keep the reply.
- **Funding is stated more often** in Canada, sometimes as a guaranteed minimum. Note whether a figure is before or after tuition.

### Study permits in 2026: two changes worth knowing (facts, not advice)

- **No provincial letter for graduate students.** Starting January 1, 2026, if you'll attend a master's or doctoral degree program at a public institution, you don't need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL or TAL). Quebec still has its own requirements. [canada.ca: graduate students](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/graduate-student/how-to-apply.html)
- **About 2-week processing for PhD students** who apply online from outside Canada with a complete application and give biometrics within 2 weeks of the instruction letter. The clock starts once your university verifies your letter of acceptance. [canada.ca: faster processing](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/graduate-student/faster-processing.html)

These rules change. Check canada.ca on the day you apply. This kit is not immigration advice.

## 11. Supervisor emails that get answered (advice)

**Find people, not just programs.** Use faculty pages, the group's own website (look for "accepting students" or "open positions"), and papers from the last two years. Shortlist 2 to 4 people per university.

**The 6-sentence email:**

> **Subject:** Prospective PhD student (Fall 2027): [specific topic] in your group
>
> 1. Who you are: degree, institution, expected graduation.
> 2. The specific work of theirs you read, and one genuine connection to your own work. Don't add a quiz-style question they'd have to research to answer.
> 3. Your most relevant research experience and what *you* did in it.
> 4. One skill or result that would help their group (a technique, code, a dataset, a measurement).
> 5. The ask: "Are you planning to take new PhD students for Fall 2027, and would you be open to my application?"
> 6. Thanks, with CV and transcript attached as PDFs named Lastname_CV.pdf.

**Cadence:** one polite follow-up after about 10 days if you get no reply, then move on. Log every email in the workbook's **Supervisor outreach** tab; it calculates the follow-up date.

**Don'ts:**
- no mass emails with swapped names;
- no long life stories;
- no attachments over a few MB;
- no generic praise ("your esteemed research");
- no request for a video call in the first email. If they're interested, they'll suggest one.

## 12. Statement of purpose: a framework for any STEM field (advice)

Programs set their own length and prompt rules. Read each prompt and obey it exactly.

1. **Opening (2–3 sentences):** the research question that drives you, stated concretely ("how disorder changes spin dynamics in…", "why graph neural networks fail on…", "how root microbiomes respond to drought…"), not "I have loved science since childhood".
2. **Research experience (40–50% of the text):** for each project, give the question, *your* contribution, the method, the result, and what you learned, including what went wrong and how you fixed it.
3. **Skills and preparation:** techniques, instruments, code, datasets and advanced courses that map onto their work.
4. **Why this program:** two named faculty and why, plus facilities, centres or rotation options that matter for your plans.
5. **Goals:** where the PhD leads and why this training fits.

**Before you submit:** every sentence should be true and specific, and you should be able to defend it in an interview. Write it yourself. Never use purchased or ghost-written statements.

## 13. Recommendation letters: the request kit (advice)

- **Who:** research supervisors first, then instructors of advanced courses who know your work. Programs differ on number (some want exactly two, most want three). The Atlas notes list it where a page states it.
- **When:** ask at least 4 to 6 weeks before your earliest deadline, and send portal invitations early.
- **What to send (one email, one PDF):** your shortlist with deadlines and portal links (export it from My plan), CV, draft statement, a "brag sheet" (projects, dates, what you did, skills shown) and a reminder of specific moments they saw your work.
- **Reminders:** a friendly note 10 days and again 3 days before each deadline. The workbook's **Recommenders** tab tracks status.

## 16. Money, safety and what this kit does not cover

- **Scams:** be wary of anyone who guarantees admission, sells "special" fee-waiver codes, or asks for your portal passwords. Real waiver codes come from the program itself.
- **Paying fees:** many portals take Visa or Mastercard credit cards, and some accept debit cards processed as credit. Enable international online transactions with your bank before the deadline week.
- **Visas and study permits:** out of scope. Use only official sources: travel.state.gov for US student visas, gov.uk for UK student visas and ATAS, canada.ca (IRCC) for Canadian study permits, the Australian Department of Home Affairs, and for Europe the host university's international office and the national immigration authority it points you to.

## 17. How this data was built (and how to report an error)

- Each of the {{N}} programs was checked against its department page and its university's graduate-admissions pages; the newest check was on {{CHECKED}}. Every value stores the exact sentence it came from, the URL and whether the university or the department set the rule. A script confirms that each quote appears verbatim on the fetched page.

- A weekly automated check re-reads the cited pages and flags quotes that disappear. Updates go to buyers as new versions at the same download link, at no charge.
- Some sites block automated checks. Programs that couldn't be verified are left out rather than guessed, and are added in updates once their pages can be read.
- **Found something wrong or changed?** Reply to your purchase receipt with the program and the page link. Confirmed corrections ship in the next update.
